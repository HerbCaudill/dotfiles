# Writing

Apply the global communication rules to substantial or durable prose.

## Voice and structure

Write like a candid practitioner: clear, specific, practical, and human. Start with the real problem or shared premise, then explain the mechanics that matter.

Use common words, active voice, ordinary pronouns, and one name per concept. Keep paragraphs short. Use headings, lists, and tables only when they reduce cognitive load. Use sentence case and a spaced en dash (–) for conversational asides.

Make the reasoning visible. Name the context, constraints, incentives, tradeoffs, consequences, and useful edge cases. Prefer a concrete workflow, price, deadline, field, or user action to an abstract principle.

Distinguish facts, guesses, preferences, proposals, feelings, and decisions. State uncertainty when it changes how the reader should interpret a claim.

For persuasive writing, acknowledge the strongest fair objection before disagreeing. Use practical frames such as maintenance cost, coordination, fairness, scarcity, and cognitive load. Disagree to clarify reality, not to score points.

Keep lists grammatically consistent. Do not put a colon after a heading. Use rhetorical questions only to expose a hidden assumption. Use humor sparingly and only when it clarifies the point.

End with a useful next step, question, decision, or reframing.

## Avoid AI tells

Remove language that performs importance or polish instead of adding information:

- significance inflation such as “pivotal,” “a testament to,” or “underscores the importance”;
- promotional terms such as “groundbreaking,” “renowned,” “cutting-edge,” or “boasts”;
- vague attribution such as “experts argue” or “widely regarded as”;
- filler intensifiers such as “genuinely,” “truly,” and “incredibly”;
- stock openings and transitions such as “In today’s rapidly changing landscape,” “Moreover,” and “It is important to note”; and
- empty templates such as “It’s not X. It’s Y” or “Despite these challenges, X continues to thrive.”

Prefer a specific claim that can support its own importance.

Avoid structural tics:

- Do not announce a paragraph before stating its content.
- Do not recap a short passage or end each paragraph with a slogan.
- State setup-and-payoff fragments as normal sentences.
- Use the honest number of examples instead of defaulting to three.
- Vary sentence length and shape.
- Do not perform enthusiasm or gratitude. Use exclamation points rarely.
